## Why the seven-core ontology survives S1

The literature screened at taxonomy level supports seven distinguishable problem families:

- selective processing/attention and memory-control interfaces;
- motivation, valuation, affect and goal pursuit;
- self/group/social influence;
- judgment and choice;
- persuasion, belief, credibility and resistance;
- linguistic meaning construction;
- deliberate rhetorical/message organization.

These families overlap. The ontology therefore uses **primary membership + secondary memberships + scientific edges**, not mutually exclusive compartments.

## Major boundary decisions

### Attention ↔ Motivation
Attention decides what receives processing priority. Information seeking and goal pursuit are motivational unless the node specifically concerns attentional allocation/control.

### Motivation ↔ Judgment
Expected value and affect can shape choice, but choice rules/reference dependence remain C4.

### Social/Identity ↔ Persuasion/Trust
Identity, norms and affiliation remain C3. Trustworthiness/credibility judgments about a source are primarily C5.

### Judgment Framing ↔ Rhetorical Framing
C4 represents the psychological representation/reference effect. C7 represents how a communicator constructs comparative/contrast language. Do not duplicate the same node across both.

### Language ↔ Rhetoric
C6 explains how expressions are parsed and interpreted. C7 explains how communicators arrange claims, evidence, narrative, contrast, refutation and action requests.

### Persuasion ↔ Rhetoric
C7 is message architecture. C5 is the receiver-side belief/attitude/trust/resistance process. A rhetorical tactic is not itself proof that persuasion occurred.
